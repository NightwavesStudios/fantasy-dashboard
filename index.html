How the trade calculator works

Check market fairness first, then Rankings / Needs for roster fit. Trades always use raw FantasyCalc values (not VORP).

Why raw market value (not VORP)?

Trades clear on what managers will accept. FantasyCalc reflects consensus dynasty/redraft prices for your format (1QB/SF, PPR, team count). VORP is better for ranking team strength; summing VORP can undervalue depth pieces people still pay for. Most trade tools sum market values and judge fairness by the gap.

Fairness rule

Each side is the sum of selected asset values. The meter shifts by the gap as a share of the larger side:

  • Within ~8-10% of the larger side → effectively even (common fair band in trade calculators)
  • Larger gaps → the meter leans toward the side receiving more value

There is no hard winner stamp. Context (needs, schedule, dynasty horizon) still matters.

Power ranking impact

Below the meter, both teams are re-scored as if the player swap already happened (picks are ignored for lineup strength). You get each team's new Power score, the change, and where they would rank vs the rest of the league. Record and weekly scoring stay as they are today.

How power rankings work

Power is a weighted blend of roster quality and results. Weights move with the season: early weeks trust starters/bench more; later weeks trust scoring and record more. The snapshot above the table shows this week's exact percentages.

VOR columns are diagnostics. Bench in Power is not raw VOR.

1. Starter strength (column vs Power)

Column “Starter strength” = sum of FantasyCalc values on the optimal legal lineup (one slot each; FLEX/SF not triple-counted).

Power’s starter input = slot surplus on that same lineup: Σ max(0, V − RV_slot). Raw total is shown for readability; surplus is what enters Power so near-replacement FLEX fillers do not inflate rank.

2. Bench strength

Everyone not in the optimal lineup. Each player is multiplied by a position weight (RB/WR 1.00, TE 0.50, QB 0.25 SF / 0.15 1QB, DST/K 0.10), sorted by weighted value, then a diminishing curve 1.00 → 0.80 → 0.65 → 0.50 → …. That stops a random QB3 from inflating bench vs real RB/WR depth. Score is market quality vs other benches, not VOR vs starter RV.

3. Positional surplus (columns / needs)

VOR vs external positional replacement. Scarcity diagnostic only. Not summed as starter strength and not the definition of bench.

4-6. Scoring & record

Avg always when points exist. Median needs ≥2 weeks for a separate Power signal (else falls back to avg). Record: (W+1)/(G+2).

Power

Roster (starter + bench) vs. results (avg/median/record) is split 80/20 within roster and 55/20/25 within results, but the roster/results balance itself shifts across the season:

  • Week 0-1: roster is about 70% of Power, results about 30%. There's little or no game data yet, so paper roster strength is most of what you have.
  • Each additional week shifts about 4.5 points from roster to results.
  • By roughly week 13, results already carry the clear majority; by the end of a full season roster bottoms out around 12% and results (record + scoring) drive ~88% of Power.

The idea: early on, projected roster strength is the best information available. As actual games pile up, what a team actually did should matter far more than what it should have done on paper - a team that outperformed its roster all year deserves to rank above one that looked strong on paper but underperformed. Scores are normalized (median + MAD, then tanh, clamped to 0-100).

How team needs work

Each position is split into Starters, Bench, and Overall so you can see whether a gap is in the lineup or only behind it.

Buckets

  • Starters - optimal legal lineup at that position (same assignment as Rankings). VORP mode uses surplus vs replacement; Regular uses market value.
  • Bench - same idea as Rankings bench: market value of non-starters (streamable positions weighted down). Not VOR vs starter RV (that would zero almost every backup).
  • Overall - starters metric + bench metric

Labels

Each bucket vs the league median for that bucket. Need ≈ < −12% · Avg · Strong ≈ > +12%.

Simulate power impact

Pick My team, then click Simulate power impact. For each candidate (top ~15 per position), the app tries adding that player and dropping each active roster player in turn (including starters, not only the worst bench piece). IR and taxi players are skipped as drop targets because cutting them does not free a normal roster spot. Only swaps that improve your Power score are shown, with the best drop and the Δ Power for that move.

This only recomputes your starter/bench numbers and re-normalizes against the rest of the league as it stands; it does not model other teams' moves. Sort by Power impact to see the biggest gains first.

Playoff picture

Current seeding plus simple odds from simulating the rest of the regular season.

Seeding

Sorted by record, then points for (common default). Division rules are applied when the league reports divisions; otherwise it is a pure overall ranking.

Odds

Each remaining week is simulated many times using each team's average score and score variance so far. Playoff spots and byes follow your league's playoff team count and bye structure. Title odds run a simplified bracket from those seeds. This is a rough model, not a forecast market.

Expected wins, luck & SOS

All-play expected wins and real-schedule strength of schedule.

Expected wins: each week your score is compared to every other team’s score (all-play). Your all-play win% times games played is how many H2H wins a neutral schedule would “expect.”

Luck: actual wins minus expected. Positive means you won more than your scores usually would.

SOS: average points/game of every opponent you have played or still have on the schedule. Higher means a tougher path.

Strength of schedule

Week-by-week projected path using best lineups, H2H + league median as two games per week.

Final weeks use real scores. Future weeks build each team's optimal lineup (byes out; empty slots filled from waivers). H2H% and Med% are win odds; the record stacks into a decimal W-L by the last week.

League ranking: #1 is the easiest schedule (lowest average opponent points).

Playoff path

Which remaining games move your playoff odds the most.

Uses the same Monte Carlo as Playoff odds. Each card shows how your playoff % changes if you win or lose. SOS supplies projected win rates and remaining schedule context.
